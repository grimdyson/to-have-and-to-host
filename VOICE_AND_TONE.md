# Voice and Tone

Wedding copy should feel celebratory, lightweight, upbeat, intentional, welcoming, clear, concise and warm.

The goal is useful information with personality, not decorative copy for its own sake.

## Write like this

- Lead with what guests need to know.
- Prefer short sentences and familiar words.
- Keep headings direct.
- Preserve the couple’s natural phrasing when it is already clear and distinctive.
- Use the couple’s regional spelling, date style, time style and terminology.
- Make practical instructions specific enough to act on.
- Sound warm without becoming overly sentimental.

Preferred:

> Join us for dinner, drinks and dancing.

Avoid:

> We are absolutely overjoyed and beyond excited to invite you to join us for an unforgettable evening filled with endless love, laughter, cherished memories and magical moments.

## Avoid

- Generic AI-generated introductions
- Excessive adjectives
- Long romantic preambles
- Wedding clichés unless the couple asks for them
- Overly whimsical headings
- Forced humour
- Corporate or transactional language
- Repeating the same sentiment in multiple sections
- Filling space because the layout has room
- Gendered clothing instructions unless the couple explicitly wants them
- Unconfirmed accessibility, transport, timing or venue claims

## Editing user responses

Treat onboarding answers as source material, not publish-ready copy.

- Remove repetition, filler and conversational fragments.
- Distil long answers without changing their meaning.
- Do not invent missing facts.
- Do not introduce sensitive details merely because they appeared in a longer answer.
- Do not assume pronouns, titles, cultural conventions or relationship labels.
- Confirm ambiguous or consequential details before publication.
- Use first names in the hero unless full names are explicitly requested there.
- Keep full names only in approved formal or metadata contexts.
- Show the proposed section copy to the user before marking that setup stage complete.

Editorial judgement should clarify the couple’s voice, not replace it.
